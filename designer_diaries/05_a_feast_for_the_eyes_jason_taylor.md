## About Jason Taylor

I've been a graphic designer and art director since 1996. I started my career in Florida, working in the theme park industry under some truly talented ex-Imagineers, who always taught me to focus on the idea and let that lead the way to the execution. As such, I've worked in many different arenas — from motion graphics for TV to games, where I started with Hasbro in 2001. Working in tabletop games has been by far the most rewarding experience, particularly with this talented and extremely creative Restoration Games team.

## Starting with a Mood Board

When we start a new project, my job is to provide the direction for the visual elements. Often, as was the case with Return to Dark Tower, it starts with a mood board. We use the mood board to discuss the themes and key aspects we want to highlight — to spark discussion among our own team and to on-board prospective artists.

Do we want something dark and gritty or whimsical? What colors do we want to dominate the palette? For this game, we were already looking at dark colors punctuated by gold and other metallics. We were also looking at the use of geometrical shapes to distinguish between the forces of good and evil, something we would continue to flesh out over the course of the project.

## Concept Art — Investing in the Vision

Another thing we do on a big project like this is invest in concept art. It might seem like a waste to pay an artist for work that's not going to be in the game. On the contrary, concept art is invaluable in giving focus to the project. It helps us start to clarify the world and ask the right questions visually.

We initiated the concept phase with **Garrett and JJ Ariosa** (with whom we previously worked on Stop Thief!). In addition to being great illustrators, they are both amazing concept artists because they can work quickly and iterate a single concept in diverse ways.

At the concept art stage, we're not thinking about the final art at all. These concept illustrations are beautiful in their own right, but it's about helping us develop the visual story of the game while the narrative itself is still being written.

![Four Kingdoms Concept Art by Garrett](images/post_2745371_feast_for_the_eyes_jason_taylor/2d9816fc9a21785ed383a4d5fe061fc3_original.jpg)

For the four kingdoms piece, it lets us ask: *Is this right for Return to Dark Tower? Why or why not?* The visuals almost immediately expose just how much we know (or still need to uncover) about the world we're creating. What is the relationship between the Tower and the lands that surround it? Pieces like this help us answer questions like that.

*(As it turned out, we liked Garrett's world-building so much we tasked him with the board illustration.)*

## Concept Art for Heroes

The roles the players will take are critical to the play experience, so we start concept art on the heroes as well. I provided Garrett a brief on the very rough idea of their powers and abilities. Garrett will draft a number of different approaches — different styles and themes, but also different genders, body types, and ethnicity. At the outset, we don't necessarily set out to have a specific character be any one thing, instead narrowing that down as we finalize the visuals of all of them at the same time.

## From Concept to Final: Qistina Khalidah

Once we finalize the concept design, we turn it over to our lead illustrator **Qistina Khalidah**. We keep a running directory of artists we find from all over. We had seen some of Qistina's work online, and once we started building out the world, we knew she would be perfect for the project.

We provide a brief background story for the heroes. You can see how a small detail — such as a bell — can end up in the final piece. It's important to let Qistina explore the character as well, so she has free rein to develop them further.

![Hero Concept — Qistina Early Work](images/post_2745371_feast_for_the_eyes_jason_taylor/da3f1b878596fc753ced718a67df5d3e_original.jpg)

One of the great things about working with Qistina — much like a concept artist — she never stops thinking about the character. As you can see in her own iterations, she's visually asking us more questions about who the character is. And as an art director, it's always fun to have options.

We'll follow this same process for all of the heroes. You can see that we envisioned the Spymaster to be, appropriately, a cipher from the beginning — an inscrutable identity beneath their garb. You can also see how Qistina adds some amazing, playful details from her early pencils to the final piece, such as the half-moon belt clasp and the climbing claws.

![Spymaster Concept / Final Art](images/post_2745371_feast_for_the_eyes_jason_taylor/7998750a94221b8e79453375708ed9b7_original.jpg)

## The Foes — JJ Ariosa's Work

While Garrett was working on the heroes, we tasked JJ Ariosa to develop the evil forces of the land. JJ's creative work for the foes covers a wide range of creature concepts, and then Qistina breathes life into those concepts for the final illustrations.

![Foe Concept Art by JJ Ariosa](images/post_2745371_feast_for_the_eyes_jason_taylor/87fa5f54bc225b8c2be429eb5988c62c_original.jpg)

![Foe Final Art by Qistina](images/post_2745371_feast_for_the_eyes_jason_taylor/aa3efdc32f33dd786b46f2375adfda71_original.jpg)

In addition to the foes, JJ also did the first concept work on the Tower that you saw developed in Tim's design diary — including the first ideas of using basalt and combining sculpted textures with organic materials.

## Jason's Own Contribution

I even jumped on the concept art for a bit. As we arrived closer to the narrative behind the Tower and the aspects we were liking from JJ's and Tim's work, I wanted to explore details that would help add interest and a bit of imposing doom to the structure — such as adding vertical line elements to imply height.

![Jason's Tower Detail Concepts](images/post_2745371_feast_for_the_eyes_jason_taylor/b3f9db5091624adb17906be5fee075b6_original.jpg)

And as we discussed the narrative around the seals, it became clear that we wanted these doors to feel like they were sealed shut — that it's *bad* if they were to ever open. This is where I introduced the concept of the **chained door**.

![Chained Door Concept](images/post_2745371_feast_for_the_eyes_jason_taylor/8fec7d76c83a41ad942ce665574a733c_original.jpg)
